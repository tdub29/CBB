# Analytics Twitter Threads

Turn raw analytics into Twitter threads people actually read, share, and bookmark.

## When to Use This Skill

- Drafting a Twitter thread from data analysis or model results
- Turning a chart, table, or stat into a tweet or short thread
- Planning a multi-day Twitter content series from analytics work
- Writing hooks for statistical insights
- Formatting numbers and comparisons within the 280-char constraint
- Deciding whether to use a chart image vs. text-only stat

## Foundational Rules

### The Reader Contract

Analytics threads succeed when every tweet answers: **"Why should I care?"**
Raw numbers are not content. Numbers plus context, surprise, or stakes are content.

### The 2-Second Rule

71% of thread readers drop off by tweet 3. Your hook tweet lives or dies alone in the timeline — it must work WITHOUT the rest of the thread. Spend 50% of your drafting time on tweet 1.

If the thread builds toward a current-season application, signal that payoff in tweet 1. Tell readers to stick around for what the analysis says about this year's field.

### One Stat, One Idea Per Tweet

Each tweet carries exactly one number or one comparison. If you need two stats, that is two tweets. Dense tweets get skipped.

## Thread Architecture

### Standard Analytics Thread (6–10 tweets)

```
Tweet 1 — HOOK: Surprising stat, bold claim, or question
Tweet 2 — CONTEXT: Why this matters / what prompted the analysis
Tweet 3–7 — REVEALS: One insight per tweet, building in impact
Tweet 8 — THE TWIST: The counterintuitive finding or biggest surprise
Tweet 9 — TAKEAWAY: So-what summary, 2–3 bullet recap
Tweet 10 — CTA: Engagement ask (see CTA Patterns below)
```

### Quick-Hit Thread (3–4 tweets)

For a single chart or one strong finding:

```
Tweet 1 — HOOK + the stat (attach chart image if applicable)
Tweet 2 — CONTEXT: What the number means, comparison to baseline
Tweet 3 — IMPLICATION: What to do with this info / prediction
Tweet 4 — CTA (optional, can fold into tweet 3)
```

### Multi-Day Series Thread

For campaigns (e.g. "12 Days of Madness"):

```
Tweet 1 — SERIES HOOK: "Day X of [Series Name]: [Topic]"
          Bold stat or question as the scroll-stopper
Tweet 2–5 — REVEALS: Insights, one per tweet
Tweet 6 — CALLBACK: Tie to yesterday's topic or tomorrow's preview
Tweet 7 — CTA: "Which stat surprises you most?"
```

Keep numbering consistent across days (`Day 7/12: Defense`). This trains followers to expect and look for the next one.

## Hook Formulas for Analytics Content

Every hook below is designed around data. Pick the formula that fits your finding.

### 1. The Shocking Stat

Lead with a number that feels wrong or extreme.

```
"Only 3 teams in the tournament field have a top-25 SOS AND 25+ wins.

That number was 9 last year.

Here's what it means for your bracket: 🧵"
```

### 2. The Contrarian

Challenge conventional wisdom with data.

```
"Everyone says defense wins championships.

The data says that's only half true.

I looked at every champion since 2008 — here's what actually predicts a title run:"
```

### 3. The Prediction Reveal

Share what your model says, frame it as a reveal.

```
"My model has simulated the tournament 10,000 times.

The team with the best chance to win it all isn't a 1-seed.

Here's the full breakdown: 🧵"
```

### 4. The Ranking / List

Numbered lists with a clear scope.

```
"The 5 most dangerous 12-seeds in tournament history all had one thing in common.

This year, two teams fit the profile:"
```

### 5. The Before/After

Show how a stat changed across time or conditions.

```
"Houston's defense this season: 87.2 AdjDE (1st)
Houston's defense in March last year: 94.1 (cost them the title)

What changed? 🧵"
```

### 6. The Question

Pose the question your data answers.

```
"Does 3-point shooting actually matter in March?

I pulled every tournament game since 2008.

The answer is more nuanced than you think:"
```

### 7. The "I Looked At…" Authority Opener

Establish credibility via scope of analysis.

```
"I analyzed every NCAA Tournament game since 2008 — that's 1,000+ games.

Here are the 4 stats that actually predict upsets: 🧵"
```

## Formatting Stats in 280 Characters

### Do's

**Round aggressively.** `87.2 AdjDE` is fine. `87.1923` is noise.

**Use comparisons, not absolutes.** "3rd-best in the country" lands harder than "91.4 offensive efficiency."

**Lead with the human meaning, follow with the number.**

```
✅ "Duke gets to the free throw line more than any team in the field.
   FT Rate: 42.1% (1st)"

❌ "Duke FT Rate: 42.1% (1st in the field)"
```

**Use arrow symbols for change:**

```
Houston AdjDE: 87.2 → 94.1 (March collapse)
```

**Use line breaks to create pacing:**

```
The tallest team in the field: UConn (EFFHGT: +2.3)
The shortest: Creighton (EFFHGT: -1.1)

Guess which one has the better rebounding margin?
```

### Don'ts

- Don't cram two stats into one sentence
- Don't use decimal places beyond one (unless the audience expects it)
- Don't say "interestingly" or "it's worth noting" — just state it
- Don't open with a definition ("AdjDE measures…") — weave it in or skip it

### Formatting Symbols

| Symbol | Use for |
|--------|---------|
| →      | Change, progression, before/after |
| •      | Bullet lists |
| ✅ / ❌ | Do/don't, pass/fail |
| 📊 / 📈 | Sparingly — chart callout, visual cue |
| 🧵      | Thread indicator on hook tweet only |
| 1/ 2/  | Tweet numbering |

## Chart & Image Strategy

### When to Use a Chart Image

| Scenario | Use image? | Why |
|----------|-----------|-----|
| Comparing 5+ teams on a metric | Yes | Text can't hold that many comparisons |
| Single stat comparison (A vs B) | No | Text is punchier for 1:1 |
| Trend over time | Yes | Line charts are intuitive |
| Ranking / leaderboard | Either | Short list = text; 10+ = image |
| Correlation or scatter | Yes | Can't describe a scatter plot in text |
| Model output / bracket picks | Yes | Screenshot or formatted table image |

### Image Specs for X/Twitter

| Format | Dimensions | Notes |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| Single image (16:9) | 1600×900 or 1200×675 | Best for charts |
| Square (1:1) | 1200×1200 | Good for comparison grids |
| Max file size | 5 MB | PNG or JPG |
| Min font on chart | 28px | Readable on mobile |

### Chart Design for Twitter

- **Dark backgrounds** (dark gray/navy) stand out in the timeline
- **Limit to 3–4 colors** max; use your accent color for the key finding
- **Title the chart with the insight**, not the variable name
  - ✅ "Houston's defense is historically elite"
  - ❌ "AdjDE by Team, 2025-26 Season"
- **Always include source attribution** in small text at the bottom
- **Add alt text** for accessibility: describe the key takeaway, not every data point

## Sports Analytics Conventions

These patterns are specific to the CBB / March Madness analytics community.

### Terminology the Audience Knows

Don't define: AdjOE, AdjDE, KenPom, T-Rank, Barthag, seed line, SOS, NET, WAB, efficiency margin, tempo

Define briefly (first use): EFFHGT, TORD, 3PRATED, FTR, OPOREB%

### Community Norms

- **Cite your source.** "Per Torvik" or "via KenPom" is expected.
- **Show your work.** Link to the data, describe the sample, mention the year range.
- **Embrace uncertainty.** "The model gives them a 34% chance" is credible. "They WILL lose" is not.
- **Bracket context is king.** Every stat should connect back to "what does this mean for March?"

### Seasonal Rhythm

| Window | Content type |
|--------|-------------|
| Nov–Jan | "Early signal" threads — what the data says so far, disclaimers about small samples |
| Feb | Conference play deep dives, bubble watch analytics |
| Selection Sunday week | Bracket reactions, seed analysis, matchup threads |
| Tournament week(s) | Daily picks, upset alerts, model vs. reality updates |
| Post-tournament | Retrospectives, model accuracy, lessons for next year |

## CTA Patterns for Analytics Audiences

Analytics audiences engage differently than general Twitter. They bookmark heavily, quote-tweet with their own takes, and reply with counter-arguments.

### Best CTAs by Goal

| Goal | CTA |
|------|-----|
| Engagement / replies | "Which stat surprises you most?" or "Who's your pick to bust a bracket?" |
| Bookmarks / saves | "Bookmark this before you fill out your bracket." |
| Follows | "I'm posting one of these every day through Selection Sunday. Follow along." |
| Quote tweets | "Drop your bracket dark horse below 👇" |
| Series continuity | "Tomorrow: Defense. Which defensive stat matters most in March?" |

### CTA Don'ts

- Don't ask for retweets directly ("Please RT") — it reads desperate
- Don't stack multiple CTAs ("Like, RT, follow, bookmark") — pick one
- Don't end on a CTA with zero value — pair it with the thread summary

## Thread Drafting Checklist

Before posting, verify:

- [ ] Hook tweet works as a standalone (cover the timeline test)
- [ ] Every tweet has exactly one idea
- [ ] Stats are rounded and contextualized (rank, comparison, or "why care")
- [ ] Tweets are numbered (1/, 2/, … or 1/N, 2/N)
- [ ] Line breaks create breathing room (no walls of text)
- [ ] Chart images are 1600×900 or 1200×675, font ≥28px, dark bg
- [ ] Alt text on every image
- [ ] Source cited (per Torvik, via KenPom, etc.)
- [ ] CTA is specific, single, and matched to your goal
- [ ] Thread is ≤10 tweets (unless it's a true deep-dive; max 15)
- [ ] Proofread for typos — analytics audiences will notice

## Posting Strategy

### Timing

| Slot | Window (ET) | Why |
|------|-------------|-----|
| Morning peak | 8–10 AM | Highest overall reach |
| Lunch | 12–1 PM | Second engagement spike |
| Evening | 6–9 PM | Sports audience online |

For CBB content during March, **evening (6–9 PM ET)** aligns with when games are on and the audience is most engaged.

### Engagement Velocity

The first 30 minutes determine whether the algorithm shows your thread to a wider audience. After posting:

1. Reply to the first 3–5 responses immediately
2. Quote-tweet your own hook with a one-line summary the next day
3. Pin the thread if it's your best recent work

### Series Cadence

For multi-day campaigns, post at the **same time every day**. Consistency trains the audience to look for your content. Tease tomorrow's topic in today's final tweet.
