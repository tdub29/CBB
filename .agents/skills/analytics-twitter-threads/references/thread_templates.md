# Thread Templates

Copy-paste skeletons for common analytics thread types. Replace bracketed placeholders with your data.

---

## Template 1: Single-Stat Deep Dive

Best for: One metric, explored across teams or across time.

```
1/ [Metric] might be the most underrated stat in March.

Here's why — and which teams it favors this year: 🧵

2/ First, what is [Metric]?

[One sentence plain-English definition.]
[One sentence on why it matters in the tournament specifically.]

3/ The top 5 teams in [Metric] this season:

→ [Team 1]: [Value] ([Rank])
→ [Team 2]: [Value] ([Rank])
→ [Team 3]: [Value] ([Rank])
→ [Team 4]: [Value] ([Rank])
→ [Team 5]: [Value] ([Rank])

4/ But raw numbers don't tell the full story.

When you adjust for [factor — e.g. SOS, conference strength], the list shifts:

[Attach chart image or adjusted ranking]

5/ Historically, teams in the top [N] of [Metric] advance to at least the [Round]:

[X]% of the time since [Year].

That's [comparison — e.g. "20 points above the field average"].

6/ The outlier this year: [Team].

They rank [Nth] in [Metric] despite [surprising context — low seed, weak conference, etc.].

Watch them in Round 1.

7/ The team to worry about: [Team].

They rank [poorly] in [Metric], and historically teams at that level exit by [Round].

Their matchup against [Opponent] exposes this.

8/ TL;DR:

• [Metric] is a strong predictor of [outcome]
• [Top team] leads the field
• [Outlier team] is the sleeper
• [Vulnerable team] could be in trouble

Bookmark this before filling out your bracket.
```

## Template 2: Matchup Breakdown

Best for: Previewing a specific tournament game or region.

```
1/ [Team A] vs [Team B] is the most interesting [Round] matchup.

Here's what the numbers say: 🧵

2/ The tale of the tape:

[Team A]          vs          [Team B]
[AdjOE rank]                  [AdjOE rank]
[AdjDE rank]                  [AdjDE rank]
[Tempo]                       [Tempo]
[Key differentiator]          [Key differentiator]

3/ The edge for [Team A]: [Specific strength]

[1-2 supporting stats]
[Why this matters in this specific matchup]

4/ The edge for [Team B]: [Specific strength]

[1-2 supporting stats]
[Why this matters in this specific matchup]

5/ The X-factor: [Variable — e.g. turnover margin, FT rate, 3PT variance]

[Team with the advantage] wins this category by [margin].

In games where this gap exists, the better team wins [X]% of the time.

6/ My model says: [Team] [Win probability]%

But the market/seed says: [Team] favored

The gap tells you [interpretation — value bet, expected chalk, etc.]

7/ Bottom line:

If [condition], [Team A] wins.
If [condition], [Team B] wins.

Who do you have?
```

## Template 3: Historical Pattern / Upset Predictor

Best for: Seed analysis, historical trends, upset indicators.

```
1/ Since [Year], [X]% of [Seed]-seeds have lost in the [Round].

But the upsets aren't random. There's a pattern: 🧵

2/ I looked at every [Seed vs Seed] matchup since [Year].

Sample: [N] games.

[Attach chart — win % by seed or upset rate over time]

3/ The [#1] predictor of an upset: [Stat]

[Seed] teams that [condition] upset their opponent [X]% of the time.

Without it? Just [Y]%.

4/ Predictor #2: [Stat]

[Brief explanation + supporting number]

5/ Predictor #3: [Stat]

[Brief explanation + supporting number]

6/ This year's upset candidates based on these signals:

→ [Team] ([Seed]-seed): Hits [N] of 3 criteria
→ [Team] ([Seed]-seed): Hits [N] of 3 criteria
→ [Team] ([Seed]-seed): Hits [N] of 3 criteria

7/ And the "safe" favorites — teams that match zero upset indicators:

→ [Team] ([Seed]-seed)
→ [Team] ([Seed]-seed)

8/ One caveat: these are probabilities, not certainties.

The model gives [upset candidate] a [X]% chance. That's elevated but not guaranteed.

Fill out your bracket accordingly.

Which upset are you picking? 👇
```

Here are the results — some will surprise you: 🧵

[Attach bracket image or summary graphic]

2/ Championship probability:

→ [Team 1]: [X]%
→ [Team 2]: [X]%
→ [Team 3]: [X]%
→ [Team 4]: [X]%

The field (everyone else): [X]%

3/ Final Four most likely:

[Region 1]: [Team] ([X]%)
[Region 2]: [Team] ([X]%)
[Region 3]: [Team] ([X]%)
[Region 4]: [Team] ([X]%)

4/ The biggest surprise: [Team] at [X]% to make the [Round].

Why? [1-2 stat reasons]

The market has them at [comparison — lower seed, not talked about, etc.]

5/ The model's least favorite 1-seed: [Team]

[X]% chance they don't make the Elite Eight.

The vulnerability: [specific stat weakness]

6/ Best value pick (high ceiling, low attention): [Team]

[Key stats]
[Matchup advantage in early rounds]

7/ My actual bracket picks (yes, I go against the model sometimes):

[Link or "DM me"]

## Template 5: Daily Series Post (e.g. "12 Days of Madness")

Best for: One topic per day in a countdown series.

```
1/ Day [N]/[Total]: [Topic]

[Hook stat or question]

The [topic] numbers say a lot about who survives March: 🧵

2/ Why [topic] matters in the tournament:

[1-2 sentences connecting the stat to tournament outcomes]
[Historical backing — "Since 2008, teams in the top X of [metric]…"]

3/ This year's leaders:

[Attach chart or table image]

Top 5:
→ [Team]: [Value]
→ [Team]: [Value]
→ [Team]: [Value]
→ [Team]: [Value]
→ [Team]: [Value]

4/ The team that stands out: [Team]

[Why — unusual combination, improvement, matchup relevance]

5/ The team to worry about: [Team]

[Why — weakness in this area, historical precedent for early exit]

6/ How this connects to yesterday's topic ([Previous Topic]):

[Brief tie-in — "Teams strong in both X and Y advance to the S16 at [rate]"]

7/ TL;DR on [Topic]:

• [Bullet 1]
• [Bullet 2]
• [Bullet 3]

Tomorrow: [Next Topic]. [One-line tease]

Which team's [topic stat] surprises you?
```

## Template 6: Quick Chart Post (Single Tweet or 2-tweet)

Not everything needs a thread. For a strong chart with a clear takeaway:

```
[Insight as the headline — not the variable name]

[Attach chart]

The takeaway: [One sentence interpretation]

Source: [Torvik / KenPom / your model]
```

Or as a 2-tweet thread:

```
1/ [Surprising stat or question]

[Attach chart]

2/ What this means for March:

[2-3 bullet interpretation]
[Optional CTA]
```
