Source: `SOS tweet.pdf` -> `docs/twitter_plans/sos/sos_day_1_thread_draft.md`

It's that time of year! Welcome to 12 Days of Madness, from now till the tournament I'll post on one CBB analytics topic per day based on data from Barttorvik.com.

Day 1: Strength of Schedule & Conferences

### Tweet 2
Metrics used include Avg Barthag per Conference, ELITESOS - a Bart Torvik derived metric measuring Strength of Schedule "compiled with sacred data and secret formulas".

### Tweet 3
First I looked at Conference strength over time to understand how conference strength has evolved to the current state.

After the portal era began, we see the SEC's rise, the ACC's volatility, and a growing disparity between Power conferences and mid-majors.

On the other side, we see a negative relationship for seeds 7-10 suggesting that middling Power conference teams are vulnerable.

## Day 2: Size

Source: `size tweet.pdf` -> `reports/size_post_viewer.html`

### Tweet 1
Interestingly, EFFHGT has grown consistently over time.

The number of dominant big men has not.

### Tweet 2
When controlling for seed, plotting EFFHGT over time illustrates an irrelevant predictive effect on a team's overperformance in March.

In fact, teams with outlier EFFHGT (in both directions) seem to run into trouble early.

### Tweet 3
The past two years have been slightly favorable towards big teams. But the 5 years prior were not.

Ultimately, nothing very meaningful here.

### Tweet 4
The relationship between height and success varies slightly across seed groups.

For 1-2 seeds, it seems to be a positive. But the tallest teams (>82.5) have never gone all the way.

For 3-6, it is the opposite.

For 9-10 seeds, height may help.

For the rest, we see no trend.

### Tweet 5
Despite 1-2 and 9-10 seeds benefitting from height, those with dominant bigs have not seen the same benefit. Suggesting that perhaps backcourt length is more crucial.

Seeds 3-8 and 11-13 seem to thrive with a dominant big.

## Day 4: Turnovers and Blocks

Source: `turnover and blocks.pdf` -> `reports/turnovers_thread_viewer.html`

### Tweet 1
Day 4 of the 12 Days of Madness analytics series: Turnovers and Blocks.

Everyone says March is about not beating yourself.

I looked at every NCAA Tournament from 2008-2025 to see when ball security and getting shots off actually changes bracket outcomes.

### Tweet 2
Metrics used include:
- `TOR` = offensive turnover rate.
- `TORD` = defensive turnover rate forced.
- `BLK%` and `BLKED%` are the block rates on each side.

All sourced from barttorvik.com

### Tweet 3
As usual, lets look at the trends of TO's and blocks over time for anything interesting.

Here we see TO rate on a continued steady decline, while blocks are becoming more frequent.

### Tweet 4
Next, looking at how these stats relate to March overperformance after controlling for seed:

Turnover management carries over on both offense and defense.

The effect of Block rate is basically zero, while avoiding blocks is the strongest signal of the 4.

### Tweet 5
On the defensive side we see turnovers forced being the stronger signal throughout the sample but trending down in recent years. While the effect of blocks is trending up.

On the offensive side, both are consistently positive indicators of overperformance.

### Tweet 6
When looking at success in March across seed lines, we consistently see managing turnovers to be a positive. Only one seed group on the offensive and defensive sides shows up negative. Thus we can accept this as a general trend.

### Tweet 7
As we saw before the trend of blocks is inconsistent, including across seed groups.

Whereas the teams that avoid blocks seem to benefit across seed lines for the most part.

### Tweet 8
Teams with low TOs on offense tend to overperform, including when they also force TOs at a high rate.

Teams that only force turnovers well, without limiting them on offense, don't perform better or worse.

Teams that can't force or prevent TOs tend to struggle.

### Tweet 9
This view would suggest that forcing turnovers is more important than limiting them, when predicting who will win it all.

Interestingly, there don't appear to be any champs who played an exceptionally low TO style. This could hurt HOU, Illinois, Bama, Arkansas, Wisco.

### Tweet 10
Former champs are all over the map for blocks, nothing conclusive there.

Straight away we see McNeese again avoiding blocks well, which we established as a strong signal.

Other notable teams who avoid blocks include, the Zags, Nova, Purdue, Houston, Miami OH, UF.

## Day 5: Three-Point Shooting

Source: `3pt tweet.pdf` -> `reports/three_point_thread_viewer.html`

### Tweet 1
Day 5 of the 12 Days of Madness: 3-point shooting.

Does 3pt shooting matter more in March?

Stick around till the end to see how the expected field of '26 stacks up.

### Tweet 2
Metrics for Day 5:

3PRATE: the rate at which a team takes 3s
3P%: the rate at which a team makes 3s
3P%D: the rate at which opponents make 3s against that defense
All sourced from barttorvik.com.

### Tweet 3
Unsurprisingly, the field now takes far more 3s than it did in 2008, while the increased shots have come at an efficiency cost.

This charts illustrates how there are large differences in how 3-pointers affect the game today vs 2008, and our analysis should respect that.

### Tweet 4
Over the full sample, none of the three metrics is a huge standalone seed-adjusted edge.

Whereas, once looking in the modern 3-pt era, we see the insight strengthen for 3pt % on both offense and defense.

### Tweet 5
Looking at the trend directly shows variation and an inconsistent story over time.

While it seems 3pt % helps on O and D a little more in the modern era, the trend still varies year to year.

### Tweet 6
Looking at the 3pt metrics by seed in the modern-era, we see 3p%D the most consistent across seed lines. While all of them appear important for top seeds.

### Tweet 7
Here we look at whether shooting more 3's or less is helpful for strong shooting teams.

This reveals that if you do shoot a high volume of 3's, you better shoot consistently. And it looks like those with very high rate and % tend to benefit the most.

### Tweet 8
Former winners have rarely shot the 3-ball at a high rate, casting doubt on the ceiling of the red teams.

Whereas Iowa State and Purdue sit in a danger zone of top seeds to get upset round 1.

### Tweet 9
Plotting former champs doesn't give any insights on former champs. But earlier we saw 3pt D may be important, especially for high seeds like Duke and Michigan.

Whereas Northern Iowa could give a 3pt dependent matchup some trouble.
