# Robustness Checks: Uncertainty on the Headline Descriptive Claims

Generated by `scripts/robustness_checks.py` (10,000 resamples per test, fixed seeds). Every point
estimate was first re-derived with the same loaders and table builders that wrote the original reports;
the `matches_published` column in each CSV records whether it agreed to 6 decimals.

## Bottom line

- **(a) SOS within seed line:** rho `+0.061` (n = 1088), 95% CI [-0.001, +0.123], permutation p = 0.041: borderline (permutation and bootstrap disagree at 5%). Shuffling only among
  same-seed teams in the same season gives a null centred at `+0.031` (~52% of the estimate) and p = 0.290, so much of it is season-level
  co-movement that 17 tournaments cannot pin down.
- **(b) Seed-band correlations:** 0 of 131 survive Benjamini-Hochberg at q < 0.05 (0 at q < 0.10). 9 have nominal p < 0.05, against ~6.6 expected by chance. Smallest q = 0.367 (sos Elite SOS, seeds 11-13).
  The quoted size numbers (EFFHGT `+0.108` seeds 1-2, `-0.077` seeds 7-8) have q = 0.909 / 0.909.
  The published player-experience band values (e.g. `+0.554` for seeds 1-2) come from a
  row-misalignment bug; with rows aligned the largest |rho| is `0.108`.
- **(c) Conference overperformance:** Horz `+0.420` has median `-0.118`; Butler's 4 appearances sum to `+7.588`, more than the conference's entire `+7.559` total. Without Butler the mean is `-0.002` (p = 1.000).
  Horz (p = 0.026, q = 0.422) and MWC (`-0.342`, p = 0.018, q = 0.422) are not significant after BH across 32 conferences, and as the most
  extreme of 32 means they are unremarkable (P = 0.201 / 0.220). Cochran's Q permutation p = 0.271: no detectable conference effect beyond seed.
  Empirical-Bayes shrinkage puts every conference within `0.023` of zero.

## Method

- **Bootstrap CI (teams):** 95% percentile interval, resampling team-seasons with replacement *within their exact
  seed line* (seed lines keep their size), re-estimating any seed-line centering inside each resample.
- **Bootstrap CI (seasons):** same estimator, resampling whole tournaments. Outcomes inside one bracket are coupled,
  so prefer this interval when it is wider. For conferences a third variant resamples programs.
- **Permutation p:** the stat is shuffled *within exact seed line* (outcomes and seeds fixed), so the null keeps all
  seed structure. Two-sided (`2 x` smaller tail, +1 correction). `p_perm_seed_season` shuffles only among the four
  same-seed teams of one season, which also holds season-level shifts fixed.
- **Multiple comparisons:** Benjamini-Hochberg across all 131 seed-band cells in (b), and across the 32 conferences in (c).
- **Conference shrinkage:** normal-normal empirical Bayes (DerSimonian-Laird). A conference mean's sampling variance is
  the sum of its teams' seed-line finish variances over n^2, i.e. its variance under the within-seed null.

## Caveats

- These are descriptive checks on 17 tournaments (2008-2025, no 2020). They ask whether a published number stands
  out from seed-structure noise, not whether it would predict future brackets.
- Percentile bootstrap intervals can under-cover in small, skewed samples (e.g. Horizon: n = 18 with two extreme
  values). The permutation p-values do not rely on that approximation.
- In seeds 14-16, 90%-92% of teams share their seed line's most common finish, so a single deep run moves the
  estimate and bootstrap distributions can be bimodal (e.g. experience coach_tourn_games_5y: `+0.208`, CI [-0.320, +0.268]). Trust the permutation p there.
- Cells overlap (tempo since 2014 is a subset of tempo all years; offense/defense metrics share teams), so the
  tests are dependent. BH controls FDR under positive dependence; since nothing survives BH, nothing would survive the stricter Benjamini-Yekutieli correction either.
- The empirical-Bayes interval treats tau^2 as known (plug-in), so it is approximate.
