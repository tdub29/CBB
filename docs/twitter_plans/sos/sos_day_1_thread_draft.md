The question: does a tougher schedule actually help teams make deeper March runs?

## tweet 2

Metrics used include Avg Barthag per Conference, ELITESOS - a Bart Torvik derived metric measuring Strength of Schedule "compiled with sacred data and secret formulas".

## Tweet 3
First I looked at Conference strength over time to understand how conference strength has evolved to the current state.

After the portal era began, we see the SEC's rise, the ACC's volatility, and a growing disparity between Power conferences and mid-majors.

Attach: `reports/figures/sos_conf_strength_over_time.png`

## Tweet 4
Of course a tough strength of schedule correlates strongly with deep runs, but does it predict them?

After controlling for exact seed line, the relationship weakens significantly.

This casts some doubt as to how much "battle tested" really matters on its own.


Attach: `reports/figures/sos_vs_finish_pooled.png`

## Tweet 5
That said, it is interesting to look at the relationship over time.  

In particular, we have seen all-time highs in back to back years.

Is this a trend of significance now that teams have fully adjusted to life in the portal? That remains to be confirmed, but it is worth keeping in mind.

Attach: `reports/figures/sos_vs_finish_by_year.png`

## Tweet 6
So when does SOS actually matter?

To answer this, I seperated the seeds into groups of 2-3, ignoring 14-16 seeds. We see the strongest trend by far in the 11-13 seed band. Suggesting battle-tested low seeds are dangerous.

The next strongest is the 1-2 lines, indicating that top seeds benefit from a tough SOS.

On the other side, we see a negative relationship for seeds 7-10 suggesting that middling Power conference teams are vulnerable.

Attach: `reports/figures/sos_vs_finish_by_seed_group.png`

## Tweet 7
Now that we've looked at the application of SOS, lets circle back to conferences. 

Which conferences thrive and which fail to meet the (one shining) moment?

The MWC has historically struggled but stablized in recent years. While the ACC and BE seem to hit their stride in March. And it looks like bball certainly didn't help the Pac12.

!!!be sure to note power conf legend

Attach: `reports/figures/sos_conf_vs_overperformance.png`


## Tweet 9
Now for the fun part, what does this say about 2026?

Like I mentioned before, it is worth weighting SOS lightly in all cases given the trend of the L2 years.

But more specifically:

Conferences: CUSA: Watch out for Liberty if they get through, CUSA is no stranger to madness.
ACC: They've overperformed in March consistently this past decade.

High seeds:
Michigan and Duke seem the best prepared for a deep run.
Uconn is Uconn, but their SOS is 4.5% lower it was when they won as a 1 in 2024.
Arizona, and Florida are in reach.
Can Illinois and Purdue put it together for 6 Wins, unlikely, but they're battle tested and it may show.

Middle seeds: Texas may not be able to cash in their experience.
Don't be concerned about Saint Louis' schedule.

Low seeds:
If VCU, USF, or Santa Clara make it, watch out.
On the other hand: if SMU, Missou, or Indiana are in it, don't be surprised by an early L even if they're favored.
